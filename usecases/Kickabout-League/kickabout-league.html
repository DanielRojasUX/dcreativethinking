Case Study Kickabout League
kickabout league

A Sunday league with the live stats of a pro club.

Role : Founder, Product Design, Web and AI Systems Builder
Org : D Creative Thinking
Product : Kickabout League, an app to create, run and follow soccer leagues and tournaments
Status : Designs complete, launch pending
AI partner : Claude
Abstract

Amateur players pay their fees, wear club quality kits and show up every Sunday like it is a final, yet their league lives in group chats and on paper. Kickabout League gives them what the pros get: their name on the table, their own numbers and a match followed live. The referee records each goal and card by tap, by voice or from her watch, and every phone, the standings and the admin's dashboard update within a second. The designs are complete, and early user testing came back positive.

My part

I own the product. I defined the positioning, the four roles and the league rules, set the visual direction, chose the type and the colors, directed the photography and the motion, and designed all 41 screens across the phone, the Apple Watch and the desktop, with five clickable prototypes.

AI in the process

Claude built the prototypes in HTML from my direction, generated the sample league data, measured contrast and wrote the image prompts, which I generated and selected myself. I reviewed every screen and corrected it round after round: photos with passion instead of generic ones, modern kits, one stripe per screen, numbers centered like a shirt, "vs" in one column, the watch, and the captain who also plays.

01 / Pro Treatment

Pro
Treatment

  • What the players already doThey pay a fee every week, wear club quality kits with printed patterns, keep a captain, a number and a position, and show up at 8 PM on a cold Sunday as if it were a final. The passion is professional. The record is not: results live in group chats, cash and a sheet of paper.
  • The ideaKickabout League is not a league manager first. It gives amateur players the illusion of professionalism they already act out: their name on the table, their own numbers and a match that is followed live, like the pros.
  • Your name up thereThe scorers list, the fair play table and the standings carry real names and real form, updated the second the referee records a goal or a card.
  • Your own numbersDiego opens Me and sees his season like a player card: goals, assists, matches, cards, minutes and how far he is from a ban.
  • A match you can followThe match center reads like a TV graphic: crests, score, minute and a timeline of every goal, card and sub.
02 / Four People

Four
People

  • One record, four doorsEveryone looks at the same league from their own job. What the referee records is what the player, the captain and the admin see. Nothing is retyped.
  • Diego, the playerOpens the app for the score, the table and his own numbers. He can change his photo; his shirt number and name belong to his captain.
  • Beto, the captain, who also playsRuns the roster and the money, and plays on the same team. A Captain and Player switch under his name swaps the whole app between his panel and his own player view.
  • Marisol, the refereeRuns the match from her phone or her watch: clock, score, goals, cards and subs, by tap or by voice.
  • Rosa, the league adminWorks on a computer. She creates the league, sets the rules, builds the calendar, follows the money and settles disputes.
03 / Visual Language

Visual
Language

  • Photography with passionNight football as it is lived: floodlights, haze, neighborhood pitches and modern kits in deep colors, never the same twice. The front of the app is a clear game hero in cool light; the screens inside use warm film, closer and more personal. Lime never appears in a photo; it belongs to the interface.
  • A title screen, like a gameThe app opens on the number 10 mid strike, the mark, the name typed in letter by letter and "Tap to start", with live league news running along the bottom.
  • One stripe per screenThe main box of every screen runs edge to edge with square corners, like a broadcast bar, with a fine lime light along its top edge. Everything else is frosted glass.
  • Numbers like the back of a shirtEvery number sits centered in its own space: stats, scores, money, minutes and shirt numbers. In fixture rows "vs" always sits in the same column; a long team name is cut and shown whole on tap.
  • Type and colorNexa for text, Poppins for numbers. Lime #EDFC5A leads the actions on charcoal #191919; each person keeps a tint, and cards keep their real yellow and red.
04 / The Live Match

The Live
Match

  • Tap, team, numberMarisol taps Goal, Yellow, Red or Sub, picks the team and the shirt number, and records it at the current minute. Suspended players never appear in her number pad.
  • The phone becomes the cardA yellow or red card fills the screen with the number and the name, so she can show it if she forgot her cards. Showing it is recording it.
  • Say itShe can speak instead: "Yellow card, number ten, Halcones". The phone proposes the event and she confirms with one tap. Nothing is recorded from voice without that tap.
  • Everyone, at onceThe same event lands in Diego's match center and stats, the live table, Rosa's dashboard and the public league page.
  • Closed by both sidesAt full time both captains sign the report. If one disagrees, it goes on hold and Rosa decides.
05 / On the Wrist

On the
Wrist

  • Why a watchA referee runs, whistles and holds cards. Her phone is in a pocket. The watch keeps the whole live match on her wrist, with every feature of the phone.
  • The number on the crownShe taps the event and the team, then turns the Digital Crown to the shirt number. The number sits large and centered, with the previous and next numbers above and below.
  • The card on the wristA yellow or red card fills the watch with the number, so she can raise her wrist and show it.
  • Voice, too"Goal, nine, Tigres": the watch proposes it and she confirms or says it again.
  • Typing goes to the phoneA note is the one thing a wrist cannot type. "Add a note" opens the note on her phone and the watch says "Continue on iPhone". Ending the match takes a press and hold, so it never happens by accident.
06 / Rules in the App

Rules in
the App

  • Points you can changeWins give 3, draws 1 and losses 0 by default. Rosa can change any value and the season table recalculates as she types, before she saves.
  • Numbers belong to the captainOnly Beto sets shirt numbers and names. Numbers already taken on his team are locked in the picker.
  • One team per leagueA player who already plays for another team in the league cannot be added; the app says who and where.
  • Suspensions apply themselvesBy default five yellow cards or one red keep a player out of the next match. The app locks him out of the lineup.
  • Money with a clear ownerCaptains collect in cash or in the app and mark who paid; they pay the referee and the league from the app.
07 / Motion

Motion

  • A spotlight on the touchThe box you touch squashes and stretches, then glows while everything around it blurs and fades for a moment.
  • Liquid tabsTabs, the tab bar, the admin menu and the Captain and Player switch share one pill. It stretches until it covers both options, thins out like a drop, then pulls into the new one with a small bounce.
  • Screens in stepsThe old items leave first. Then the blurred background slides: left going forward, right going back. Then the new items arrive one by one, slightly out of step.
  • Motion specThe pill takes 620 ms. Items leave in 240 ms, the background moves in 700 ms, and new items start at 300 ms, about 62 ms apart. Under reduced motion everything resolves at once.
08 / Accessibility

Accessi­bility

  • Contrast, measuredEvery text pair was measured against WCAG 2.2 AA with the glass composited over charcoal, after every color change: the lime, the charcoal and the small capital labels were checked again.
  • A tap for every gestureSlide to pay and slide to sign arm on the first tap and confirm on the second, and Enter does the same from the keyboard (WCAG 2.2, 2.5.7). On the watch, the crown also works by swipe and by the arrow keys.
  • States in wordsPaid, unpaid, suspended and live always carry a label, so color is never the only signal.
  • Cut names, never lostA team name cut to keep "vs" aligned keeps its full name for screen readers and shows it on tap.
  • Reduced motionUnder reduced motion every transition resolves at once, the name appears without typing and the ticker stops.
