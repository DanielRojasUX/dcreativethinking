Case Study 01 Dashboard Landing

Asked for a UI update, I pitched a dashboard. Daily usage rose 20%.

Role : Senior Lead UX Designer
Org : IBM / Kyndryl
Product : Kyndryl Bridge
Abstract

Kyndryl Bridge is the multi cloud management platform Kyndryl's enterprise clients work in. Its landing page was a static list of links used regularly by under 5% of users. The project was a UI update. After listening to the teams and the users, I pitched leadership a bigger bet: a personalized dashboard built from widgets that every Bridge application could contribute. Leadership backed it, and after launch daily usage rose 20% and task completion time dropped 35%.

01 / The Problem

The
Problem

Kyndryl Bridge is the multi cloud management platform Kyndryl's enterprise clients work in. Its landing page was a static link farm meant for new users, and under 5% used it regularly: most people bookmarked the apps they needed and skipped it.

Returning users got nothing from it. No shortcuts to their own workflows, just the same repetitive steps every time.

Under 5% regular usage
02 / Research

Research

I started by listening: delivery partners, product owners, UX designers and development managers first, then the users themselves.

What I heard reframed the brief. People did not need a better list of links; they needed a starting point built around their own work.

03 / The Decision

The
Decision

  • Why links could not workBridge applications run independently but share accounts, users and permissions. A static list could not adapt to that many use cases.
  • The betI pivoted the project into a dynamic, personalized dashboard. The tradeoff: a bigger, more resource intensive build that needed leadership to back it.
  • The pitchJourneys for each persona showed where navigation broke down. Low fidelity mockups of the dashboard won leadership buy in; mid fidelity in Figma added customizable layouts, multiple dashboards and permission aware sharing.
04 / Delivery

Standardization
& Delivery

Engineering teams across every Bridge application built the widgets. I coordinated that multi team effort and wrote the widget guidelines and process that kept every application consistent.

05 / Reflection Kyndryl Bridge

Looking
Back

What went well?

The pitch changed the scopeLow fidelity mockups turned a static UI update into a user centered dashboard project.

Listening shaped the featuresInterview insights drove the personalized features that answered the under 5% usage.

Shared standards heldClear widget guidelines kept many teams and applications consistent.

What would I do differently?

Share the roomI led the internal playbacks. Inviting team members to co facilitate would have spread ownership and grown leadership across functions.

Plan the follow upA formal post launch monitoring framework would have brought user feedback in faster and kept the dashboard improving.

02 / Research

Research

Subject matter interviews

I interviewed delivery partners, product owners, UX designers and development managers about user needs, platform pain points, and how the applications were managed and used.

User interviews

Users confirmed the low usage of the landing page. Their real workflows let me redefine the user journeys and exposed the navigation problems and inefficiencies.

Patterns across personas

Mapping journeys across the personas showed the common paths, the frequent tasks and the usage patterns. All of them pointed to the same need: a personalized, efficient starting point.

04 / Delivery

Delivery

Working across teams

Product owners and engineering teams across every Bridge application built and shipped the dashboard over a year long, multi team effort. I coordinated it and taught the teams how to create and use widgets.

Widget guidelines

I established the widget design guidelines and documentation, so widgets from different applications looked and behaved as one system.

A process, not a one off

I defined a clear process for widget design and development, with approval workflows and quality assurance checks, so new widgets kept to the standard after launch.
