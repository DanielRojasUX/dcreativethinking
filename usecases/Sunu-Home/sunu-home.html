Case Study Sunú Home

One photo of your fridge, and dinner is decided.

Role : Founder, Product Design, Web and AI Systems Builder
Org : Sunú, with D Creative Thinking
Product : Sunú Home, a kitchen app that turns what you have into what you cook
Status : Under testing
AI partner : Claude
Abstract

Every evening most homes ask the same question in front of the fridge: what can I make with what I have? Sunú Home answers it from a photo of the fridge and the pantry, starting with the food that needs using first. The same photo tells people where each thing keeps best and what is about to run out, and the grocery list builds itself, compares the stores near them in Aguascalientes and plans one route. It is a Sunú product, now under testing.

My part

I own the product with Sunú. I defined the problem, the five jobs and the one way setup, set the bold visual direction from my own reference board, chose the type and the rule that each recipe takes the color of its dish, picked the real stores and markets, and designed the 32 screens and their motion.

AI in the process

Claude built the prototype in HTML from my direction, drew the flat illustrations, wrote the sample recipes and checked every screen at phone size. I reviewed it round after round and corrected it: a sign in and a one way setup instead of a welcome screen, every step required with nothing assumed, colors that follow each dish, and Sunú present in the setup and at the key moments.

01 / The Question

The Question
at the Fridge

  • Every evening, the same questionA fridge half full, a pantry with a little of everything and the same thought every night: what can I make with what I have? Most homes answer it by buying more, cooking the same three dishes or throwing food away at the end of the week.
  • The ideaSunú Home answers that question from a photo. You snap the fridge, the pantry or both, and it tells you what you can cook tonight, starting with what needs using first.
  • Three jobs around one kitchenThe same photo also tells you where each thing keeps best, and what you are about to run out of. The grocery list builds itself from your recipes, compares the stores near you and plans one route.
  • Why SunúSunú is a home food brand in Aguascalientes whose promise is cooking with love. Sunú Home carries that promise into the customer’s own kitchen, with Sunú recipes in the mix and a Sunú pasta tray one tap away on the nights there is no time to cook.
  • Under testingSunú Home is a Sunú product in testing. The prototype behaves as the live app will: real stores in Aguascalientes, real recipes and a full setup.
02 / One Way Setup

One Way
Setup

  • Sign in, then the wizardAfter the splash there is one door: continue with Apple, Google or email. It leads straight into seven steps with no exits, no skips and no defaults.
  • Nothing is assumedEvery step starts empty. Continue stays locked and says what is missing, like Add your name to continue or Pick one, or None of these, until the person answers.
  • Easy to answerArc dials for people at home and minutes on a weekday, color tiles for restrictions and equipment, a slider for the weekly budget and real stores to pick from. Typing is only asked once, for the name.
  • A profile that pays offThe last screen turns the answers into a kitchen profile with big numbers, the restrictions as chips and the reminders to turn on, under the Sunú curve and the cocina con amor mark.
03 / Visual Language

Visual
Language

  • A bold proposal, built from referencesThe direction came from a board of poster work: flat fields of saturated color that reach the edges, stacked rounded cards with a pill tag, one giant curve used as a container and book covers that open into the page.
  • No photos, so color tells the dishRecipes have no pictures. Each cover is a flat poster in the dish’s own color: green for chilaquiles verdes, chipotle brown for tinga, a chipotle cream peach for the pasta, tomato orange for sopa de fideo and yolk yellow for huevos a la mexicana.
  • Tonal pairsText takes a deeper or lighter tone of its own field instead of plain black or white, so every card reads as one object.
  • TypeBig Shoulders Display for titles and the giant numbers, Poppins for everything else, with light lowercase lines for the questions the app asks. No italics anywhere.
  • Sunú, where it belongsThe real Sunú logo signs the sign in, every setup step, the finish screen and the weekly email. The Sunú curve appears once per screen, cropped so it never reads as a letter.
  • English first, Spanish built inEvery screen switches between English and Spanish from the sign in or the profile.
04 / Scan and Cook

Scan and
Cook

  • Fridge, pantry or bothThe camera asks for the fridge first, then moves to the pantry by itself after the first shot. Corner guides and a short hint keep every shelf in frame.
  • Reading the kitchenA scan line sweeps the photo while each ingredient gets its box and its name. The count climbs, and the screen floods red with the result: ingredients found, recipes you can make and what to use soon.
  • People confirm, AI proposesNothing is cooked from a guess. The ingredient list comes back as chips to remove or add before any recipe is suggested.
  • Ask it your wayWhat can I make? by text or by voice, shaped by quick filters like under 30 minutes, use soon first or kid friendly. Recipes respect the profile every time.
  • How the AI fitsA vision model reads the photos and a language model plans the recipes, each behind its own layer so either can change. The current plan pairs Gemini for vision with GPT for cooking, always inside the kitchen profile.
  • Hands busyCook mode shows one big step at a time with its own timer and moves on when you say next. At the end you rate the dish and the leftovers go back into the kitchen.
05 / A Kitchen That Makes Sense

A Kitchen That
Makes Sense

  • A score you can raiseThe kitchen tab gives the fridge a score and names the few moves that would raise it.
  • Every zone, with its whyNumbered pins open a sheet per zone: what goes there and why. Raw chicken goes on the bottom shelf so nothing drips on food you eat raw; milk leaves the door because the door is the warmest spot.
  • Before and afterA slider compares the fridge as it is with the fridge as it should be, with the days of freshness each move adds.
06 / Shop Smarter, Closer

Shop Smarter,
Closer

  • A list that writes itselfMissing items from recipes and anything running low land on the list as tilted notes grouped by aisle, each with the reason it is there.
  • Real stores in AguascalientesPrices compare across Walmart Independencia, Soriana Universidad, Chedraui Asunción, La Comer Altaria, HEB Santa Mónica and Mercado Terán. Prices in the prototype are illustrative.
  • The places with no websiteTianguis and tienditas have no prices online, so people say them: tianguis on Thursdays, avocados three for twenty. The app turns the sentence into a place and its prices.
  • One routeThe best mix splits the list between the market and one supermarket, then plans the stops, the time and the total. In store, the list becomes big check rows per stop.
07 / Before It Runs Out

Before It
Runs Out

  • Days, not guessesFrom each scan and how fast the home uses things, Sunú Home estimates the days left for each item and puts the list one tap away.
  • Reminded your wayPush notifications, a weekly email and an Apple Watch option, each turned on by the person in setup or in the profile.
  • Useful, not noisyA reminder carries the price and where to buy on the way home, or the tianguis day before it happens.
08 / Motion

Motion

  • Floods for big momentsStarting the scan, the result and the finish arrive as a color rising from the bottom of the screen, like a page being filled.
  • Covers that openA recipe grows out of its own cover into the full page, and folds back into it on the way out.
  • A liquid tab barThe active pill stretches across both tabs, thins and settles into the new one with a small bounce.
  • Items in stepsEvery new screen slides in the direction of travel and its content arrives one piece at a time.
